Q What is automated refuse collection?
A

Automated Refuse Collection facilitates safer and more efficient collection of residential trash. Standard sized and shaped trash containers are distributed to households. The containers are easily grasped by a hydraulic arm on the automated trucks that dump the trash into an overhead compartment.

Not only does automated collection reduce driver injuries associated with regular trash collection, it also increases refuse collection efficiency.

Q What are the "Additional Fees" and other exclusions in the Miramar fee schedule?
A
  • Special Handling Fees — Set at 1.5 times the Trash/Refuse fee. Examples include tree stumps, large items, large poles, large pipes, dead animals, hard-to-handle materials, dismantled boats, or recreational vehicles. The fee recovers the additional handling required for these items.
  • Fibrous Greens — Set at 1.5 times the Green fee. Additional handling and processing is required to break down fibrous plant material such as palm fronds.
  • Green Material — Fees for automated and manual greens have been consolidated. Additional handling and processing is required to break down fibrous plant material.
  • Food Material — Set at 1.5 times the Green fee. Food material is subject to stricter requirements, must be processed within 30 minutes of delivery, and must finish processing by the end of each day.
  • Clean Wood — Set at 0.5 times the Green fee. The cost of processing Clean Wood is estimated at half the cost of processing Greens.
  • Exemptions — City residents will now pay for Greens and Clean Wood since weekly automated organic waste collection is provided by the City and franchise haulers; this fee exemption is no longer necessary.
  • Metallic Discards — The Metallic Discard Waste Fee has been removed as metallic goods (refrigerators, washing machines, dishwashers, etc.) are not accepted for disposal at the Miramar Landfill — these items go to Recycling Centers.
  • Uncovered Loads — The Uncovered Load fee has been increased to $50 for all vehicles to reduce litter, increase safety, and keep San Diego clean. Per CVC 23115(a), no vehicle transporting garbage, refuse, trash, or rubbish shall be driven on any highway unless the load is totally covered to prevent any part of the load from spilling or falling.

Q What are Short-Term Residential Occupancy (STRO) Tiers, why do they matter, and where can I locate this information?
A

License requirements are not the same for all operators and depend on the tier of license obtained. There are four potential tiers a STRO can fall under. This information can be found on the license itself or by searching the City's database of active licenses: STRO License Data Set.

  • Tier 1 — Required for home share or whole home STRO for an aggregate total of 20 days or less per calendar year. The host does not need to reside onsite with a Tier 1 license.
  • Tier 2 — Required for home share STRO for an aggregate total of more than 20 days per calendar year. Only allowed in the host's primary residence. The host must occupy the primary residence for no less than 275 days of the calendar year in which it is rented as a home share.
  • Tier 3 — Required for whole home STRO outside the Mission Beach Community Planning Area for an aggregate total of more than 20 days per calendar year. A two consecutive night minimum stay by the same guest is required.
  • Tier 4 — Required for whole home STRO within the Mission Beach Community Planning Area for an aggregate total of more than 20 days per calendar year. The same guest must meet a two consecutive night minimum.

Q What things can I do to lengthen the life of my automated trash, recycling, and organic waste containers?
A
  • Keep your container in a safe area, out of direct sunlight
  • Do not load containers with more than 300 pounds of material
  • If your trash or recycling container is less than half full, consider not setting it out for collection — the container body is less likely to develop cracks if there is resistance where the automated arms grab it
  • Place containers three feet apart when setting them out
  • Make sure your trash is bagged before placing it in the container
  • When possible, place your container on level ground

Please note that the average life expectancy of containers is 10 years.

Q What is a Certified Mixed Construction & Demolition (C&D) Recycling Facility?
A

A Certified Mixed C&D Recycling Facility accepts commingled loads of most C&D debris. The materials are then sorted by the facility and marketed separately. A mixed C&D load generally includes: metals, untreated wood, inert materials, drywall, concrete, asphalt, carpet padding and foam, pallets, clean dirt, cardboard packaging, and yard waste.

Inert materials are a combination of asphalt, concrete, dirt, fines, rock, brick, sand, soil, and stone.

Q What happens to the recyclables after the City collects them?
A

The City contracts with two companies to process and market curbside-collected materials: EDCO Disposal and Allan Co. These companies operate Materials Recovery Facilities that sort commingled materials through manual and mechanical processes.

Material moves down conveyor belts past workers who pull off cardboard and contaminants, then through a disk screen and air classifier to separate containers. Magnets extract steel cans, plastics are sorted by type, and glass is sorted by color. Paper is separated into newsprint and mixed paper.

The sorted materials are marketed to manufacturers who turn them into new products:

  • Paper → new paper products, packaging, furniture
  • Plastic → containers, clothing, carpet, car parts
  • Aluminum → drink cans, aircraft, car parts
  • Steel cans → machinery, building materials, car parts
  • Glass → new containers, decorative tiles, road reflectors, jewelry
Q What is deconstruction/salvage?
A

Deconstruction, also known as salvage, can eliminate disposal costs and may result in taxable donations. During the process, hazardous materials are properly removed and reusable materials are salvaged for reuse at the same project or donated to reusable item stores, other individuals, or non-profit building organizations.

Reusable items include: wood floors, doors, electrical and bathroom fixtures, bricks and blocks, steel beams and studs, lumber, windows, tiles, and more. The Certified Recycling Facilities list and map offer a directory of San Diego recyclers that accept salvaged materials.

Remember to allow enough time at the start of the demolition phase for deconstruction — postponing demolition while construction details are worked out reduces the time available to salvage or recycle during demolition.

Q What do the report statuses mean?
A

Reports submitted to Get It Done may be categorized into one of the following statuses:

Open

  • New — Once a report is received, the status updates to "New" to indicate it was successfully submitted and has been routed to a staff member for processing.
  • In Process — Once a work order is created, the status changes to "In Process". This means work is being scheduled, has been scheduled, or is already underway. Sometimes a report becomes "In Process" if there are multiple reports for the same issue — once the initial report is closed, all related reports will also be closed.

Closed

  • Closed — Once a report has been closed in the work order system, the status changes to "Closed". For some graffiti and encampment reports, an "after" photo is provided. For other reports, a "closure reason" is provided to offer more detail about the action taken.
  • Referred — Not all reports are handled within the Get It Done system. Some may be referred to an agency outside the City (e.g., Caltrans or AT&T) or to another City department not yet connected to Get It Done. The status remains as "Referred" and is not updated for reports referred out of the system.
Q How are duplicates tagged? What should I expect if my report is tagged as a duplicate?
A

When a resident submits a report, the system automatically evaluates it for duplicates based on the report type (e.g., 72-hour violation, encampment, illegal dumping). A City employee then verifies if the duplicate tag is accurate.

The primary case in a duplicate relationship is always the first case reported. If your case is tagged as a duplicate, it will remain open and become attached to that first case. You will not receive an email notification of this update.

Once the issue is resolved, all cases linked to the first report will be closed at the same time.

Example: A resident reports a problem with a streetlight → this creates a new case. The next day, another resident reports the same streetlight → this is tagged as a duplicate and attached to the first case. When the streetlight is fixed, staff closes the first report, closing all attached duplicates simultaneously.

Q What are the 8 Signs of Terrorism?
A
  • 1. Surveillance — Terrorists may conduct surveillance to determine a target's strengths and weaknesses.
  • 2. Elicitation — A terrorist may try to gain information about the operations and security of a potential target by asking questions about a specific place.
  • 3. Testing Security — Someone may use different methods to test security, such as trespassing into a restricted area or leaving a bag unattended in a public place to see how quickly people or security respond.
  • 4. Funding — Terrorists need to raise money for their operations and spend it in a way that doesn't draw attention.
  • 5. Acquiring Supplies — To conduct an attack, terrorists may need a variety of supplies such as weapons, transportation, and communication systems.
  • 6. Impersonation — Terrorists may impersonate law enforcement officers, firefighters, EMS personnel, mail carriers, or company employees to gain information.
  • 7. Rehearsal — Terrorists often rehearse a planned attack to ensure their operation runs smoothly.
  • 8. Deployment — This is when terrorists are putting their plans into place — getting into position, moving supplies, and preparing to attack.

Learn more about the Eight (8) Signs of Terrorism.

Contact sdrecyclingworks@sandiego.gov or customer service at (858) 694-7000 for assistance.

Visit recyclingworks.com for more information on the City of San Diego Recycling Ordinance, how to comply, exemption criteria, educational signage, and lists of franchise haulers.

Q What will happen if I don't follow these requirements?
A

The responsible person(s) at the facility will be held accountable if a property is found to be out of compliance. A written warning (Notice of Violation) will be sent, and a City Code Compliance Officer will set a follow-up date. Free assistance will be offered to ensure compliance.

If non-compliance continues, the responsible person(s) may be subject to:

  • Criminal prosecution, civil injunction, or administrative abatement
  • Civil penalties and revocation of permits
  • Recordation of the notice of violation and withholding of future municipal permits

The Code Compliance Officer may also:

  • Issue an administrative citation — first offense fees begin at $50
  • Continue to issue citations not to exceed $500 per subsequent offense if compliance is not met
  • Issue a $100 re-inspection fee beginning with the third inspection
